## What you may do without asking

- **State compatibility factually.** "Compatible with omcp", "built for omcp", "an omcp server",
  "implements the Open Model Context Protocol" — accurate statements of fact about
  interoperability are always fine.
- **Refer to the project.** Use the name in articles, talks, documentation, comparisons, and
  academic work.
- **Reproduce the logo unmodified** when referring to the project — in a blog post, a slide, a
  compatibility badge, or a list of supported protocols.
- **Fork the repository.** The license grants this, and nothing here restricts it.

- Using the Marks in the **name of your product, company, service, or domain**, or in a way that

- Using the Marks as **your own** trademark, logo, or app icon.
- **Modifying the logo** — altering its colours, proportions, or elements — and continuing to
  present it as the omcp logo.
- Applying the Marks to **merchandise** for sale.

## Forks

You may fork this project, and you may say that your fork is derived from omcp. You may not name
the fork using the omcp Marks, or present it in a way that suggests it is the official project.
This is the same courtesy the fork itself observes toward the upstream Model Context Protocol
project.
